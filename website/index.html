01 / ABOUT THE ATLASWHY THIS ATLAS EXISTS

An open foundation
for BCI research.

BCI Paradigm Atlas
An open atlas of BCI paradigms and their neural foundations.

BCI experiments start with stimuli, tasks and timing. This atlas connects the tasks that elicit a signal to its neural sources and cognitive meaning. Initiated and maintained by SavorCode.

Paradigms and aliases are scattered across papers. Mechanistic assumptions are often implicit. Coverage tends to cluster around a few familiar tasks. Shared records, identifiers and evidence rules make this knowledge easier to search, compare and reuse.

The paradigm catalog records concrete experimental protocols. The neural knowledge base records markers, brain regions and cognitive constructs. Explicit relationships connect the two layers into an exportable knowledge graph.

Oddball: detecting rare targets

Frequent standard stimuli and rare targets appear in random order. Participants count targets or press a button. The record links this protocol to P3b and lists visual/auditory stimulation and EEG, MEG and fMRI recording modalities.

PROTOCOL

Two stimulus types; target detection or counting; trials combine stimuli and intervals. The full record retains parameter ranges, variants and foundational sources.

NEURAL KNOWLEDGE

P3b is linked to the temporoparietal junction (B), context updating (B) and attention allocation (B). These relationships are recorded in the knowledge base; content verification is still pending.

05 / EVIDENCE & STATUSWHAT THE EVIDENCE MEANS

Transparent evidence. Clear limits.

Grades A–D describe the strength of published evidence.
Evidence grade and review status are separate dimensions.

A

Synthesis & replication

Supported by a meta-analysis or systematic review, or replicated across independent laboratories and multiple modalities.

B

Independent studies

The relationship is supported by multiple independent studies.

C

Single or indirect evidence

Currently supported by a single study or indirect evidence.

D

Theoretical proposal

A proposed relationship that has not yet been tested directly.
